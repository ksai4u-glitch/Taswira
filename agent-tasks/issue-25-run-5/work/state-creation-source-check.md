supplement (Gazette Extraordinary No. 37, Vol. 54, Part A) at archive.gazettes.africa
contains the 1967 decree. I read it page by page (7 pages), and it gives the 12 states
and their areas. So 12 and 19 are supported by primary text. 21, 30, 36 and the FCT are
not yet supported by any primary text I could read.

## Source 2a: archive.gazettes.africa, 1976 supplement no. 14 part A

URL: `https://archive.gazettes.africa/archive/ng/1976/ng-government-gazette-supplement-dated-1976-03-17-no-14-part-a.pdf`

| Item | Finding |
|---|---|
| Opened? | Yes. 4 pages (A55-A58), read as scanned images. |
| Identity | Supplement to Official Gazette Extraordinary **No. 14, Vol. 63, 17 March 1976**, Part A. **States (Creation and Transitional Provisions) Decree 1976, Decree No. 12**. The header shows "[3rd February 1976]"; section 7 deems it in force from 3 Feb 1976; signed "Made at Lagos this 10th day of March 1976", Lt-Gen O. Obasanjo. |
| Contents | Creates states named in the Schedule. Each state is defined by provinces, divisions or districts (not by present-day states), with a capital. Section 4: the Supreme Military Council may appoint a **States Boundaries Adjustment Commission**; any adjustment takes effect only after SMC approval. Section 6(1) substitutes section 3 of the 1963 Constitution: "nineteen states", listed by name. Section 6(3) repeals the 1967 decree and its amendments (1967 Nos. 19 and 25; 1974 No. 16). |
| The 19 | Anambra, Bauchi, Bendel, Benue, Borno, Cross River, Gongola, Imo, Kaduna, Kano, Kwara, Lagos, Niger, Ogun, Ondo, Oyo, Plateau, Rivers, Sokoto. Count checked: 19. |
| Items relevant to the brief | **Kogi/Kogi area:** "Kogi" appears as a division inside **Kwara** (with Ilorin, Igbomina/Ekiti, Lafiagi/Pategi, Kabba, Igbirra, Oyun, Borgu). **Igala and Dekina Divisions in Kwara State** go to Benue. So the 1991 Kogi creation (later) draws on Kwara and Benue areas that this schedule names. **Abakaliki** is in Anambra's schedule (relevant to Ebonyi, 1996). **Abuja** appears as an area in **Niger** State's schedule. **No FCT** exists in this decree. |
| Splits inside divisions | Many states are cut through divisions or districts, e.g. "Bauchi Division less Jarawa District", "Jarawa District in Bauchi Division" given to Plateau, "Gwoza Local Authority area" and "Biu Division plus Uba District in Adamawa Division" for Borno, "Aboh less Ndoni area" in Bendel with the "Ndoni area" in Rivers, and the Opobo Division split between Cross River and Rivers. |
| Supports | **19 states.** Does not support 21, 30, 36 or the FCT. |
| Rights | **Unknown.** The site's about page returned HTTP 403. The scan carries "Published by authority of the Federal Military Government of Nigeria". I did not find a licence or a copyright term, and did not guess one. |
| Gaps | Date inconsistency (3 Feb commencement vs signed 10 March) should be noted, not smoothed over. No coordinates or maps. Boundary text is in older units (divisions, districts), which need a separate source to place on a map. |

## Still needs a source

1. **1987:** the original decree number, date and text for Katsina and Akwa Ibom (source 3 could not be opened).
2. **1991:** the decree creating nine states (Abia, Delta, Enugu, Jigawa, Kebbi, Kogi, Osun, Taraba, Yobe, by memory, unchecked), with its schedule. Kogi's components (Kwara and Benue areas) are only inferable from the 1976 schedule.
3. **1996:** the decree creating the six states and any schedule. Ebonyi's component areas, including whether Benue or Cross River areas were included.
4. **FCT:** Decree No. 6 of 1976 (Federal Capital Territory) and its boundary schedule; the date the capital actually moved (1991) from a primary source.
5. **Boundary adjustments:** any instrument issued by the 1967 or 1976 commissions, and the panel the 1996 speech mentions.
6. **Geometry:** historical province, division and district boundaries (1967, 1976) with a known licence. Public Notice No. 116 of 1951 is cited for Lagos only.
7. **Rights:** the terms of archive.gazettes.africa, policyvault.africa, UT PCL, archivi.ng and nigeriareposit; the legal status of Nigerian federal gazettes.
8. **Re-check by the maintainer:** open sources 3 and 4 in a browser; confirm the quotes from source 5 on the live page.
