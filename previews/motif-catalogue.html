Taswira · research and creative direction

Culture, memory, identity.

Eight researched directions for a richer visual vocabulary. Cultural motifs, dated objects, political emblems and new editorial designs have distinct roles. This catalogue records proposals and limits; it does not automatically place an emblem on a historical timeline.

01

Dagin Arewa / Hausa interlace

Hausa; northern Nigeria

Original cultural-frame study

A named cultural design vocabulary with older knot contexts and a separate modern political history.

Role
Cultural/editorial identifier
Evidence date
Mid-20th-century shoes; a robe catalogued Before 1910?; political adoption in the 1950s
Proposed use
Original braid can frame a Hausa cultural chapter or an attributed design vignette. Distinct Hausa cities remain distinct actors.
Boundary
No checked source establishes the supplied raster variants as medieval kingdom flags or seals. No invented meaning for each loop.

Lam Museum: Contexts · UBC Museum: baban riga · Moses Ochonu,2008

02

Edo / Benin court leopard

Edo, Kingdom of Benin

Royal art associates the leopard with the oba’s authority.

Role
Documented court motif; original interpretation
Evidence date
Checked object 1550–1680; no invention date claimed
Proposed use
Source-gated leopard profile or rosette in the Benin story.
Boundary
A royal motif does not establish a standardized coat of arms or flag. Distinguish Kingdom of Benin from modern Republic of Benin.

Met Leopard 1978.412.321 · Kathy Curnow: Kingdom-based art

03

Role

Evidence date

Proposed use
Research a particular court and period before making an original crown-based identifier.
Boundary
No universal Yoruba flag or medieval date follows from this late object. Restricted object photograph is not reused.

Met Adéńlá2025.97.1

04

Ikenga: achievement and the right hand

Specific Igbo communities and shrine traditions

The checked shrine record relates right hand, achievement and horn imagery; forms vary.

Role
Religious/material-culture interpretation
Evidence date
Checked figure: late 19th–mid-20th century
Proposed use

05

Nsibidi / ukara

Cross River region across southeastern Nigeria and southwestern Cameroon

Graphic communication crosses spoken-language boundaries; public use coexists with restricted knowledge.

Role
Regional graphic communication and association context
Evidence date
Checked textile c. 1975–1985; this is not an origin date
Proposed use
Explain communication and cloth with attributed public evidence.
Boundary
Do not label it an exclusively Igbo alphabet, invent translations or reveal claimed secret meanings.

Birmingham Museum: Textile ukara 1986.2

06

Akan; Ghana

A bird turning toward its tail supports a particular Akan idea of reflection and learning.

Role
Documented proverbial visual form
Evidence date
Checked gold weight: 18th–19th century
Proposed use

Not a generic African seal or evidence that this gold weight is an adinkra stamp.

07

Adinkra: a specific textile vocabulary

Asante-Akan

Repeated blocks and individually documented motifs have particular uses and meanings.

Role
Stamped textile design and moral expression
Evidence date
Checked cloth mid–late 20th century
Proposed use
Use researched motifs with their names and contexts.
Boundary
Do not combine signs into a fabricated universal pan-African crest.

Saint Louis Art Museum Cloth 56:1997

08

Zomo, Taswira’s fictional hare guide

Original brand character, informed by Hausa storytelling research

Proposed personality: curious, attentive and quick-witted. These are creative choices, not a fixed traditional meaning.

Role
Original editorial character; never a political emblem
Evidence date
Saifu selected hare direction on 27 October 2026
Proposed use
Guide viewer attention, open questions, acknowledge uncertainty, become still during suffering.
Boundary
Do not copy modern illustrated retellings or treat Zomo as an ancient eyewitness. Credit particular tellers when adapting actual tales.

Yusuf Mohammed,2014, Hausa tricksters · Tremearne 1913 original collection

Checked 27 October 2026. Original artwork only. Source-specific dates, passages, rights and unresolved claims are recorded in history/research/creative. Restricted museum photographs are not reproduced. The permanent mascot direction selected by Saifu is the hare; Zomo is its working name.
