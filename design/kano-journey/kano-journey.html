Hausa cities · Kano

The earth becomes a city

How sure: dates follow the Kano Chronicle, written down about the 1880s. Who finished the first wall is disputed. Why?

Before Kano was a city, it was a hill. People worked iron on Dala. The Kano Chronicle remembers Barbushe, keeper of a shrine on the hill. That memory is tradition, and it has no date.

By the Chronicle's reckoning, King Gijimasu began a wall, gate by gate. Who finished it? The Chronicle says Yusa, in the 1100s. Nigeria's heritage file says Zamnagawa, in the 1300s.

Then Muhammad Rumfa. The Chronicle says he built a palace and moved in the next year. The year after, he pushed the walls outward, gate by gate. He founded a market, Kurmi, which still trades today.

How we know · The earth becomes a city

Dates follow the Kano Chronicle

The Chronicle was compiled from older traditions and written down about the 1880s; its reign dates are a reckoning, not records. The map shows each part at its Chronicle date and says so.

Where sources disagree

Who finished Kano's first wall? The Kano Chronicle credits Yusa (1136–1194 by its reckoning); Nigeria's 2007 UNESCO tentative-list file credits Zamnagawa, in the mid-14th century.

The age of the Dala settlement also differs: the 7th century (Iliffe) or the 10th (UNESCO file). The map shows it from the later date.

What is drawn, and what is not

Gate positions, wall lines, the palace footprint and the market are schematic. Not drawn: present-day palace decoration (mostly 1930s–40s), the 1951 mosque, the 16th-century wall line.

Sources
  1. H. R. Palmer (transl.), "The Kano Chronicle", JRAI 38, 1908 Primary tradition, translated · via Wikipedia summary
  2. Murray Last, "Historical Metaphors in the Kano Chronicle", History in Africa 7, 1980 Peer-reviewed analysis
  3. UNESCO, Ancient Kano City Walls and Associated Sites (Tentative List), 2007 Heritage nomination by Nigeria's NCMM
  4. ArchNet, Gidan Rumfa Architectural history database
