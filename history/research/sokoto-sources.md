# The Sokoto Caliphate: source check (31 Oct 2026)

The chapter after the Hausa cities, from Usman dan Fodio at Degel to the British conquest of 1903.
Three scenes: the jihad (1774 to 1809), a caliphate of emirates (1812 to 1841) and the conquest
(January 1897 to July 1903).

**How the sources were seen.** Wikipedia could not be opened from the session that wrote this
chapter: the session's network blocked it. Facts from the sources added on 31 Oct 2026 were taken
from web search results that quote those pages, and each source's `checked` note says so and asks
for a re-check on the page itself. Sources already in `sources.json` (`wiki-sokoto`,
`wiki-places-key`, `wiki-battle-kano`, `wiki-battle-sokoto`, `wiki-colonial-north`, `wiki-rnc`)
were opened by earlier sessions on 27 Oct and are reused as they were checked. Wikipedia pages are
pointers to the works they cite; only facts are taken from them.

## Beats, with their sources

The dated beats use a new basis, `record`: a date from written records and the histories built on
them, with no label rule. Where sources disagree the beat uses `differ`.

**The jihad (1774 to 1809).**
- 1774–1804: Usman at Degel, in Gobir (Degel).
- February 1804: the hijra to Gudu after Yunfa turned against him (Jihad of Usman dan Fodio; Sokoto
  Caliphate).
- February 1804: named Amir al-Mu'minin at Gudu; Yunfa declared war on 21 February (Jihad of Usman
  dan Fodio; Sokoto Caliphate).
- 21 June 1804: victory at Tabkin Kwatto (Battle of Tabkin Kwatto infobox). Only the wiki and its
  mirrors were seen; Smaldone (1977), *Warfare in the Sokoto Caliphate*, should be checked.
- 1805: Zaria taken (`wiki-places-key`). 1807: Kano taken (`wiki-sokoto`).
- 3 October 1808: Alkalawa falls and Yunfa is killed (Alkalawa). One account calls it the fourth
  assault; that detail is left out.
- 1809: Bello turns the camp at Sokoto into a town (`wiki-sokoto`, `wiki-places-key`).

**A caliphate of emirates (1812 to 1841).**
- 1812: Abdullahi at Gwandu (Abdullahi dan Fodio; Gwandu Emirate, which also gives 1805 and 1809
  for the emirate's founding).
- 20 April 1817: Usman dies at Sokoto; Bello succeeds (Usman dan Fodio).
- 1817–1829: Abdullahi at first refuses to recognise Bello; eastern emirates under Sokoto, western
  under Gwandu, Bello's authority as caliph upheld (Muhammed Bello; Abdullahi dan Fodio).
- 1817–1841: emirs appointed by the caliph, with wide self-rule, pledging allegiance and bringing
  tribute each year, including enslaved people (`wiki-sokoto`). Bauchi and Adamawa are tied to
  Sokoto as eastern emirates (status approx: inferred from the east–west division, not stated for
  each emirate).
- 1823–1824 (sources differ): Afonja killed by his allies; Abd al-Salam first emir of Ilorin,
  administered through Gwandu (Ilorin; Ilorin Emirate; Gwandu Emirate).
- 1841: Modibbo Adama settles Adamawa's capital at Yola after several moves; jihad there from 1809
  (Britannica, Modibbo Adama).

**The conquest (1897 to 1903).** No dated beats: one campaign clock drives the year counter.
- January 1897: Royal Niger Company force under Goldie takes Bida and deposes the emir (The
  Spectator, 13 Feb 1897; a reprinted report of 27 March 1897; George Taubman Goldie).
- 1897: Ilorin submits (Britannica 1911, via a Wikisource mirror); another account says by force.
- 1 January 1900: transfer to the British government (`wiki-rnc`).
- 1901 Zaria, 3 Feb 1903 Kano, 15 Mar 1903 Sokoto (`wiki-places-key`, `wiki-battle-kano`,
  `wiki-battle-sokoto`; 13 March council concession in `wiki-colonial-north`).
- 21 March 1903: Lugard names Attahiru II (Muhammadu Attahiru II). Emirs who accepted British rule
  kept in office under British officers (Library of Congress country study).
- 27 July 1903: Attahiru I killed at Burmi (Burmi campaign); his own article gives 29 July or
  August, and `wiki-sokoto` gives June in its text.
