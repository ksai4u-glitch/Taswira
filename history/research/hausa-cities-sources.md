## Beats, with their sources
- **Bayajidda (oral).** Bayajidda, a prince said to come from Baghdad, killed the snake Sarki at the Kusugu well in Daura and married Queen Daurama. Their descendants founded the seven Hausa Bakwai, and Biram came from his son by Magira of Bornu (Wikipedia, Hausa Kingdoms: Salamone 2005, Hansen 2000, Lange).
- **Roles in tradition (oral, Amadu 1984 via Hausa Kingdoms):**
  - Kano and Rano: indigo, "sarakuman babba".
  - Katsina and Daura: markets, "sarakuman kasuwa".
  - Gobir: war and defence, "sarkin yaki".
  - Zazzau: slaves, "sarkin bayi".
- **Never one kingdom (Hansen 2000; storyboard).** The states were separate, with fortified capitals, and they fought each other.
- **Yaji and Rano, 1349–1385 (Kano Chronicle).** Palmer's text says Yaji "drove the Serikin Rano from Zamna Gaba, went to Rano, and reigned at Bunu 2 years." (The Wikipedia body text gives 1343 and 37 years, which conflicts.) Amadu 1984 adds that Rano never regained sovereignty.
- **Amina, 1421–1438 by the Kano Chronicle (sources differ).** In Dauda's reign the Chronicle says: "Zaria, under Queen Amina, conquered all the towns as far as Kwararafa and Nupe. Every town paid tribute to her ... Her conquests extended over 34 years."
  - Other datings: early 16th century (from Bakwa Turunku, 1492–1522 in a Zaria list); after 1576 (Abdullahi Smith; tradition gives 1576–1610).
  - Her name is not on the regnal lists, and whether she was queen is disputed.
  - Bello claimed that Kano and Katsina paid her tribute; historians dispute this for Kano.
  - The "ganuwar Amina" walls are credited to her only by popular works.
- **Burja's tribute to Bornu, 1438–1452 (Kano Chronicle).** Burja was the first Hausa king to send tribute to Bornu. M. G. Smith thought only Kano and Biram were vassals, with the tribute sent through Daura (Hausa Kingdoms).
- **Korau of Katsina, about 1445–1495 (Katsina Chronicle; sources differ).**
  - Other datings: 1492/3–1541/2 (Stewart's table) and the 13th century (a local tradition).
  - He was Katsina's first Muslim ruler and is credited with the walled town.
  - The Gobarau mosque dates from his reign, by Wikipedia's account.
- **Kano–Katsina war, 1463–1499 (Kano Chronicle, Rumfa).** It lasted eleven years and was indecisive.
- **Songhai and Kebbi, 1512–1516 (sources differ).**
  - Kingdom of Katsina: Askia Muhammad conquered Katsina in 1512, and it was a vassal of Gao until 1554.
  - Kanta Kotal (from al-Sa'di's Tarikh es-Soudan): the Hausaland campaign began in 1513.
  - Askia Muhammad I (Levtzion 1977): he conquered Katsina, Zaria and Gobir.
  - Hausa Kingdoms: that Hausaland was tributary to Songhai is marked "citation needed".
  - Leo Africanus's account is doubted (Fisher, via the Kano article).
  - In 1516 Kanta of Kebbi rebelled over his share of the spoils from the Aïr campaign and defeated the army sent against him.
- **After 1516.** The states stayed separate until the jihad of 1804–1808 brought them into the Sokoto Caliphate (map key).

## Left out, and why
- **Nupe and Kwararafa as places.** Neither has a placed capital for this period, so Amina's reach is told, not drawn.
- **Durbi ta Kusheyi.** It has no coordinate source.
- **Kanta's capitals before Surame.** They are not placed.
- **Gao.** It lies just west of the map area (0.05 W), so Songhai is told in the narration.
- **The Bornu tribute of 100 slaves from Katsina.** It came after this scene, late in the 16th century.
