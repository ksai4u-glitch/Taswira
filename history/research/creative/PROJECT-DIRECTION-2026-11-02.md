## Recommended final website: one front door, two complementary experiences

Claude should consider the final product as a coherent website rather than two separate map pages.

Recommended navigation:
- **Home:** a visual landing page establishing what Taswira is and why to explore it.
- **Visualize / Explore:** a highlights surface with historical discoveries, short previews and chapter title cards. This can initially be a section of Home; a separate page is optional.
- **History map:** the animated, sourced journey through African and Nigerian history.
- **Conflict map:** contemporary organised-violence data, with its dataset versions, update dates and preliminary labels.
- **About / Sources:** purpose, methods, uncertainty, source attribution and creator credits.

These labels and the separation of Home from Visualize are Codex recommendations, not a fixed navigation design approved by Saifu. Build them within the existing hosting approach unless there is a concrete reason to change it. Preserve existing map URLs and deep links, or provide redirects if entry paths change. Decide the entry-page arrangement before replacing the current conflict-map index.

### What the landing and highlights experience should do

1. Open with the existing forest-green, antique-gold and serif identity, an engaging animated hero and a concise explanation of Taswira.
2. Offer two immediately clear choices: **Explore history** and **Explore the conflict map**. Label their different time ranges and purposes.
3. Include short, sourced **"Did you know?"** discoveries, visual highlights and animated chapter title cards that lead to the relevant map or precise historical scene.
4. Use specific links: a Kano card should open its history chapter/scene; a conflict card should open the relevant supported map view. Do not imply unsupported filter links already exist.
5. Present a short preview with a clear next action rather than requiring a full video to play before visitors can enter either map.
6. Retain accessible text, readable phone layouts, reduced motion, visible navigation and user-controlled audio.
7. Keep factual cards linked to chapter/source records. Preserve approximate dates and disputed claims in the short copy; distinguish oral tradition from dated history.
8. Keep conflict statistics tied to their date range and dataset version. Show a clear loading/unavailable state instead of invented or stale "live" figures.
9. Credit **sai_fu** and **rahzatech**, alongside the relevant source and asset attributions.

An initial curated set of cards is sufficient. A CMS, recommendation system or redesign of the data pipeline is not a prerequisite.

### Creative recommendation: the map becomes the world

Use geographic orientation, descend into an original illustrated or stylised cinematic scene, then return to the map to explain the significance.

Examples:
- A Kano map marker becomes an earthen wall and gate; the camera enters a researched market vignette; cloth or trade goods become a route on the map.
- A deep-time geographic view becomes lava, rock, rain and landscape transformation; return to controlled geography and dates.
- A railway line becomes a short period train sequence, then returns to the network and its historical consequences.

A consistent illustrated or stylised 3D treatment may suit the project better than unrelated photorealistic inserts. Test this through reference frames and one representative moving shot before committing to a style. "Cinematic" does not require photorealism.

## Preserve the Taswira doctrine through adaptation

- Evidence determines dates, places, actors, routes, architecture, emblems and historical claims.
- Keep uncertainty, competing accounts and source precision visible in proportion to the claim. Cinematic confidence must not erase evidentiary uncertainty.
- Do not invent borders, interpolate undocumented fronts or project modern ethnic/political boundaries backwards.
- Separate archaeological evidence, written accounts, oral tradition and illustrative reconstruction.
- Source period dress, weapons and architectural phases. A generator's plausible output is not evidence.
- Keep precise map geometry, dates, labels, numerical comparisons and source credits in controlled renders or the final edit. Generated footage is unsuitable as the authority for these.
- Preserve source and asset reuse notes; attribution for creators does not replace attribution for historical data or other assets.
- Preserve the **Deep time opening** as an important part of the teaser's framing; shortening it is a creative option, removing it is not the default.
- The map/history remains an unreviewed historical draft until the documented review requirements are met. Films should not imply that review has happened when it has not.

### War, spectacle and human consequences

The current history README suppresses effects in campaign scenes. Saifu's newer feedback in [the next-session note](next-session-2026-11-03.md) asks for stylised battle effects and weapons by era, while keeping massacres, famine, enslavement and civilian suffering calm.

Claude should reconcile these rules explicitly before implementing the distinction. Keep battles readable and non-gory, source the weapons and movements, avoid implying force strength through token size, and give human consequences and aftermath their own narrative space. This handoff does not silently rewrite the existing content rules.

## Build for reuse without delaying the map

Recommended scene handoff fields, reusing existing records where possible:
- Stable chapter and scene IDs; title and geographic focus.
- Date/period and its precision; narrative beats with proposed timing.
- Source IDs, dispute/uncertainty notes and unchecked claims.
- Camera framing and meaningful actions, not just a paragraph on screen.
- Available original SVG, character, architecture and geography assets.
- What is factual geometry versus schematic or illustrative detail.
- A short pronunciation/cultural-review note where needed.
- Capture in/out points and a small set of clean reference frames.

Retain the deterministic scene clock and existing shot-manifest/source-sidecar approach. Preserve spectacle when exporting; do not flatten animation just to make capture easy. Add a genuinely composed **9:16 export mode**, larger type and phone-safe framing instead of placing a landscape recording inside a portrait black canvas. Keep titles/captions separable from clean map imagery for editing, while retaining full evidence and credits in the final deliverable.

Not every field needs a new schema now. First establish one complete, reusable chapter handoff and extend it as production proves useful.

## First animation target and production discipline

**Proposed flagship:** "The earth becomes a city", adapted from the existing Kano/Hausa chapter. This is the recommended first production target, not a completed film or a newly researched reconstruction.

Use the documented distinction between the Kano Chronicle, oral tradition and other evidence. Avoid silently reconciling disputed wall-building dates or inventing the exact medieval city layout.

Production sequence:
1. Select a short narrative and verify its essential claims against the map's sources.
2. Prepare reference images and a rough timed storyboard before generating motion.
3. Test one difficult representative shot.
4. Generate short, independently replaceable shots.
5. Assemble with controlled map animation, narration/sound, readable captions and creator/source credits.
6. Review chronology, period detail, continuity, phone legibility and the emotional treatment of events before publishing.

Revising a title, date, voice track or edit should not require regenerating the whole film. Keep accepted clips and original assets organised for later episodes.

For context only, a tool quote on 2 November was 12.5 credits for a five-second Kling 3.0 Pro clip with sound. This is not a standing price or a promise of usable output. Recheck live costs and the approved budget at production time. Website-only unlimited/free promotions must not be assumed to apply through the plugin.
