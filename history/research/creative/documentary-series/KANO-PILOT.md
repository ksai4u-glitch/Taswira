## What the review proves and what it still needs

The review combines the actual Deep Time opening, fog clearing into the city, a citywide flight, a nearer street passage, an ascent, a palace return, Arthur, a ducked score and kinetic creator/music credits. The known generated wall dissolve was removed, with a deliberate cut between stable sections. Technical playback passed. Proper-name listening, historical framing and a fuller human story remain editorial work.

The present narration is an atmospheric invitation. It does not yet explain the political and social costs behind the city. The documentary pilot should ask **how Kano's physical growth, religious authority, commerce and coercive power became connected**, while showing how later sources construct that history. Retain wonder without turning prosperity or royal grandeur into a moral verdict.

## Original pilot opening draft

Working length roughly two minutes at a reflective pace; time against a scratch read before any paid narration. Bracketed references are editorial annotations, not spoken words. This is original narration, not a quotation from the Chronicle or the inspiration programmes.

> Before the gates, there was Dala. Nigeria's heritage account places a settlement here in the tenth century. The city that grew around it would carry the name Kano. [K2]
>
> Its beginnings reach us through layers of memory. The Kano Chronicle credits Gijimasu with starting a wall. In the chronology attached to his reign, that work belongs to the late eleventh and early twelfth centuries. [K1 p.66]
>
> Who completed it? The Chronicle names Yusa. The heritage account names Zamnagawa, much later. Even a wall can hold more than one history. [K1 p.67; K2]
>
> By the late fifteenth century, the Chronicle brings us to Muhammad Rumfa: a palace, an enlarged enclosure, and Kurmi market. It remembers new forms of authority, and a city reshaped around them. [K1 pp.77–78]
>
> Yet the same account that celebrates power also describes its human cost. Under an earlier ruler, Abdullahi Burja, it tells of southern raids and people carried into enslavement. Its immense numbers require scrutiny. The captives themselves remain largely outside the story's voice. [K1 pp.75–76; editorial observation about this passage]
>
> Rumfa's reign is remembered through religious change, court institutions and a long war with Katsina. Praise and coercion occupy the same pages. [K1 pp.77–78]
>
> This is the city we are entering: a place of markets and households, belief and ambition, protection and force. Its walls survive in fragments. The history inside them has to be assembled, source by source.

The last paragraph is the documentary's interpretive invitation, not a measured reconstruction. The film must still develop ordinary life from additional sources; anonymous crowd animation cannot substitute for it. Do not invent an enslaved person's diary, a builder's testimony, or labour conditions at a particular gate.

## Picture and sound decisions

- Open directly on atmospheric Kano/land scale, with no chapter or Deep Time preamble. Introduce the location/century after the opening impact, before a specific historical claim.
- Start mist clearing over stable geometry. Establish the city and avenue before a descent; keep light direction, travel direction, architectural anchors and skyline consistent.
- Connect gate, market and palace through a native route plan. Call a generated district “Kurmi” only as an explicitly interpretive view, with placement remaining schematic.
- Landmark formation is a visual explanation of development across centuries. Date changes must be visible. No apparent real-time construction by magic should stand in for the historical process without its stylised status being clear.
- During discussion of raids or captivity, reduce the triumphal score. Use map/source details and dignified distance. Do not reuse spectacular gate erection as celebration of conquest.
- Arthur's pauses should make space for comprehension. The latest revision removes the existing 3–4.25-second music gap: allow a pre-narration atmospheric opening while the score continues.
