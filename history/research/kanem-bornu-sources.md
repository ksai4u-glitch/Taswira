# Kanem-Bornu: where each place and date comes from

Chapter: `history/chapters/kanem.json`. Checked 31 October 2026.

**How the sources were read.** This session's network policy blocked Wikipedia, Britannica and
other sites, so the new sources were read only through web-search excerpts, not opened in full.
Each new entry in `sources.json` says so in its `checked` field. Facts reused from entries an
earlier session opened in full (`wiki-kanem`, `wiki-bornu`, `wiki-trans-saharan`,
`wiki-places-key`, `wiki-places-deep`, `frobenius-kanem`, `magnavita-2025`) are marked as such
there. Everything below should be checked against the full texts before publishing.

Not placed, on purpose: Njimi (never securely located), Tié (proposed as Njimi; its coordinate
has not been checked), the Fezzan and Traghen (outside the map), Minarge and Wadai (told only).

**Lake Chad.** The background draws today's lake. No older shoreline is drawn, because none has
been sourced for these centuries. The sources seen say the lake's level rose and fell, that a small
lake in the Bodélé depression dried out about 1,000 years ago, and that the lake has shrunk to less
than a tenth of its 1960s area. The lake point has no `reach_km`, so no disc pretends to be its size.

**The move west** is a `migration` drawn faintly (`uncertain: "whole"`) from Kanem to a Bornu ring
anchored at Ngazargamu's site: the route, and where the court stopped before Ngazargamu was
built, are not known to this page. Ngazargamu itself is drawn only from the Idris Alooma scene on.

The scenes about war, enslavement and death (`bornu`, `idris-alooma`, `sayfawa-end`) are
`sensitive`. No campaign arrows are drawn: none of Idris Alooma's expeditions has a sourced, dated
route. No flag is drawn: the `kanem-bornu` emblem entry records that none is documented.
