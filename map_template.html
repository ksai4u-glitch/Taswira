About this map, methods and sources

What it shows

Each event is an incident of organised violence that killed at least one person, recorded by the Uppsala Conflict Data Program (UCDP). UCDP sorts events into three types: state-based (a government against an armed group or another government), non-state (armed groups against each other) and one-sided (an armed group or government against civilians).

How it was made

  • Events come from the UCDP GED API, version __VERSION__, for __PERIOD__.
  • Later events come from the GED Candidate releases (__CANDIDATES__). They are preliminary: UCDP may still correct, add or remove them. They are hatched in the year chart and have a dashed ring on the map.
  • Only events located to a place, within about 25 km, or to a district are drawn. Events placed only at a province, country or along a road, river or border are counted in the totals and listed under "Not on the map", but not drawn, because their point is a stand-in and would create false hotspots.
  • Deaths are UCDP's best estimates; each event also has a low and high estimate (shown on hover). The heatmap is weighted by deaths.

Keep in mind

  • Much of the death toll from large wars can sit in the events that are not drawn (for example, the 2020 to 2022 war in northern Ethiopia), so the heatmap shows where events cluster, not every death.
  • Reporting is uneven: areas with less media coverage may have fewer recorded events.

Why other sources show more violence

UCDP only counts deaths in organised conflicts: fighting between identifiable groups that kills at least 25 people in a year. A lot of violence is left out, such as banditry, kidnapping gangs, scattered communal clashes and crime, as are events with no deaths, like protests and riots. Other trackers count some or all of these, so they show higher numbers.

For example, for Nigeria in 2024, UCDP records 502 events and 3,201 deaths. ACLED, which counts all political violence and unrest, reports about 4,000 violent events and 9,300 deaths (ACCORD summary of ACLED data). The gap is mostly down to what each source counts, not which one is more accurate. UCDP answers "how deadly are the organised armed conflicts?", while ACLED answers "how much political violence and unrest is there?".
